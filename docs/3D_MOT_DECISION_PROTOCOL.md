# 3D-MOT configuration decision protocol

## Decision objective

Select the simplest experimentally buildable 3D-MOT configuration whose
capture and retention are not materially worse than the best simulated design.
The comparison must not optimize only atom count: it must distinguish loading,
stable retention, velocity/phase-space acceptance, alignment sensitivity, and
optical complexity.

The three decision families are:

1. `five_beam_gravity`: preferred experimentally if gravity plus one unpaired
   green beam can replace the missing sixth green beam without a large loss.
2. Finite four-blue-beam designs: six green MOT beams plus one upstream blue
   slowing pair and one downstream blue backstop pair. This is preferred over a
   full blue shell if it approaches the same performance with less blue optics.
3. `angled_donut`: the performance reference. It uses six green beams and six
   coaxial center-blocked blue beams, and is expected to be the most optically
   demanding design.

## Fair-comparison rules

- Use the same selected 2D-MOT survivors, global particle indices, selection
  seed, stochastic seed policy, timestep, solver, duration, gravity, and capture
  thresholds for every candidate in a comparison.
- Use 600 atoms for screening and promote only competitive candidates to the
  full 15,840-atom ensemble.
- Primary loading metric: atoms simultaneously inside the configured radius and
  below 1 m/s at 100 ms. An earlier exit does not permanently disqualify an
  atom.
- Stability metrics: the full population curve, final usable count, loss after
  the population peak, and `tau` only when an exponential tail passes the fit
  checks. “Usable at least once” is diagnostic and cannot replace the final
  count.
- Preserve per-particle masks. Report how many atoms a modification rescues,
  loses, and retains relative to its paired control; a net count alone can hide
  population replacement.
- A scan winner on a parameter boundary requires one targeted boundary check.
- Do not compare results across a geometry or capture-rule change as if they
  were generated by the same configuration.

## Evidence required for each finalist

Each finalist must have:

1. force-sign tests on both sides of the MOT center along all three axes;
2. exact verification that protected blue-dark regions are dark;
3. 100-ms loading on the shared ensemble;
4. population versus time and, when justified, a retention lifetime;
5. initial-position and initial-velocity acceptance plots;
6. representative captured and failed trajectories;
7. an ablation or paired-control result showing the role of each nontrivial
   beam group;
8. a laboratory-complexity assessment: beam count, access constraints,
   alignment burden, and sensitivity to intensity/position changes.

## Current evidence

### Full angled donut

- Shared 600-atom 100-ms control: 491/600 usable atoms (81.8%).
- Full-ensemble 100-ms result: approximately 82% usable.
- The 100-to-400-ms continuation lost only 10 of 12,989 starting atoms, so no
  defensible exponential lifetime was measurable in that interval.
- Blue-shell ablation and trajectory plots show that repeated blue access is
  causally important; the full shell substantially outperformed isolated
  pairs.

### Finite entrance gate plus downstream backstop

- Entrance gate only: 115/600 usable at 100 ms (19.2%).
- First backstop scan: best 153/600 at a +25-mm crossing and `s0=0.75`.
- Refined scan: best 188/600 (31.3%) at +35 mm and `s0=0.75`.
- The refined best point retained all 115 entrance-only successes and rescued
  73 additional atoms, with zero paired losses.
- This proves that a downstream `k_z < 0` backstop can recover overshooting
  atoms. It does not yet show that four blue beams are competitive with the
  donut: the remaining absolute gap is 303/600 atoms.
- Required next evidence: the 35/40/45-mm boundary check at
  `s0=0.5/0.75/1.0`, followed by phase-space and trajectory diagnostics for the
  selected finite-gate point.
- The boundary check confirmed a local position optimum at +35 mm: all three
  tested intensities retained 184--188/600, whereas +40 mm retained at most 137
  and +45 mm at most 127. The selected finite-gate representative therefore
  remains +35 mm and `s0=0.75` with 188/600 usable atoms.

### Five-beam gravity MOT

- Earlier force-corrected runs demonstrated nonzero capture and an accepted
  decay, but results made before the complementary green-core aperture was
  added describe a different geometry.
- The lower-green balance scan showed substantial particle-to-particle outcome
  changes with intensity even when aggregate capture changed little. Cloud
  centering is not the decision objective, but this sensitivity matters for
  robustness.
- Required next evidence: re-optimize the current aperture-corrected geometry
  with the instantaneous 100-ms criterion, then run the same phase-space,
  trajectory, and retention diagnostics used for the other finalists.
- The five-beam balance workflow now records both “usable at least once” and
  “usable at 100 ms”, and ranks candidates by the latter. Results created by an
  older version of that workflow must not be treated as final-count results.
- `studies.submit_3d_mot_five_beam_decision` performs the first corrected
  decision screen. It evaluates 12 explicit physics anchors plus a full-donut
  control on the same 600 atoms. The anchors probe lower-beam balance, green
  detuning and intensity, magnetic gradient, blue spectral strength, and two
  limited combined hypotheses. It is not a Cartesian optimization.
- The corrected screen selected the lower gradient boundary: 1.25 G/cm retained
  159/600 atoms at 100 ms versus 89/600 at the 2.5-G/cm baseline. Independently,
  changing green detuning from -20 to -25 Gamma retained 136/600. The refined
  screen therefore checks 0.5--1.5 G/cm, -20 to -30 Gamma, lower-beam s0 4--8,
  and only one combined low-gradient/red-detuning hypothesis.
- The refined screen selected lower-green `s0=4` with 170/600 usable atoms and
  85% final retention among atoms that were usable at least once. The useful
  gradient region was bracketed by 1.25 G/cm (159/600) and 1.5 G/cm (164/600),
  while lower gradients failed. The final local grid therefore varies only
  gradient 1.25/1.5/1.75/2.0 G/cm and lower-green `s0=2/3/4/5`, holding all
  blue, paired-green, detuning, geometry, and capture parameters fixed.
- The completed boundary grid selected gradient 1.4 G/cm and lower-green
  `s0=2.5`: 202/600 atoms were usable at 100 ms and 232/600 were usable at
  least once. Across five recoil seeds, this point gave 31.23% ± 1.70
  percentage points (range 29.67--33.83%), while the paired donut gave
  83.40% ± 1.37 percentage points (range 81.50--84.83%). The gradient remains
  at the tested low boundary, so the full optimization must extend below
  1.4 G/cm.

## Promotion and decision gates

A screening candidate is promoted to the full ensemble only if it passes all
force/dark-region tests and either:

- reaches at least 80% of the donut's paired 600-atom capture, or
- offers a major laboratory simplification and reaches at least 50% of the
  donut's paired capture with evidence that a small targeted optimization can
  close the gap.

These are study decision thresholds, not physical laws or laboratory-set
requirements. If neither simplified family passes, select the full donut and
use the paired ablation, phase-space, trajectory, and retention evidence to
justify the added optical complexity.

## Presentation outputs

The final decision package should contain:

- one normalized 0--100-ms population comparison for all finalists;
- one longer retention comparison for candidates still competitive at 100 ms;
- one common-scale initial phase-space acceptance figure per finalist;
- one trajectory/velocity figure explaining the dominant failure mechanism;
- one ablation or paired-rescue figure establishing why each blue beam group is
  needed;
- one summary table containing beam counts, best provisional parameters,
  loading, retention, robustness, alignment burden, and evidence status;
- a recommendation that explicitly separates simulated performance from
  practical laboratory judgement.

## Stochastic repeatability

`studies.submit_3d_mot_decision_repeats` repeats configured representatives on
the same 600 atoms across five independent recoil seeds. It reports the mean
and sample standard deviation of the instantaneous usable fraction at 100 ms.
This measures Monte Carlo repeatability, not experimental alignment tolerance,
and therefore must not be presented as a complete robustness study.
The final paired finalist repeat gave 83.40% ± 1.37 percentage points for the
donut and 31.23% ± 1.70 for the selected five-beam point. Their observed ranges
were 81.50--84.83% and 29.67--33.83%, respectively. The earlier three-family
repeat remains useful only as historical evidence for the retired finite
four-blue representative; its pre-refinement five-beam result is superseded.
