# Historical 3D-MOT study decisions

This document preserves the conclusions of exploratory 3D-MOT scripts that
predated the canonical `studies.mot_3d_campaign` workflow. Once the corresponding
one-off programs are removed from the active tree, Git history remains the
source for their exact implementation; compact tracked summaries and figures
remain as scientific evidence where available. Studies without retained
artifacts are identified explicitly as table-only decision records reconstructed
from their completed Zeus output.

These numbers are historical design evidence, not current corrected-input
performance claims. They must not be mixed with the sealed result of a new
canonical campaign.

## Donut core/shell split screen

A paired 600-particle screen of a hard shared green-core/blue-shell boundary
gave:

| Split radius | Usable at 100 ms |
|---:|---:|
| 4 mm | 132/600 (22.0%) |
| 5 mm | 6/600 (1.0%) |
| 6 mm | 0/600 (0.0%) |

The screen established that capture was highly sensitive to the split radius
and justified making it an optimized donut parameter rather than fixing it at
5 or 6 mm. The raw screen directory was Zeus-local; this table is the retained
decision record.

## Donut velocity diagnostics

Trajectory plots showed the physical role of the blue shell: successful atoms
were decelerated toward zero longitudinal velocity, while removal or
localization of particular blue-beam groups changed that slowing history. The
diagnostic supported the paired ablation conclusion that transverse cooling,
both longitudinal senses, and repeated blue-light access matter. Retained
evidence:

## Early single-pass tuning

In the earlier localized-pair geometry, the initial point captured 5/600.
Tuning blue intensity and detuning raised this to 52/600; adding the gate
position raised it to 115/600 (19.2%). This was concept evidence only: the later
canonical `yz` single-pass geometry has different beam placement and optimizes
its crossing angle and position. The result motivated retaining single pass as
the simpler candidate, not reusing the old point as a validated setting.

## Superseded `yz` baseline screen

The first `physical_seed_600_100ms` run, generated by
`submit_3d_mot_single_pass_screen.py` and merged by
`merge_3d_mot_single_pass_screen.py`, reported 0/600 usable atoms and no atom
entering the 5-mm capture region. Follow-up closest-approach diagnostics placed
all atoms roughly 61-71 mm from the MOT center, revealing a geometry/convention
error rather than a meaningful zero-capture operating point. That run is
superseded and must not be used as performance evidence. Its raw directory was
Zeus-local at
`data/validation/mot_3d/single_pass_yz_screen/physical_seed_600_100ms`.

## `yz` operating-point refinement

The operating/local-refinement programs
`submit_3d_mot_single_pass_operating_screen.py`,
`submit_3d_mot_single_pass_local_refinement.py`,
`scan_3d_mot_single_pass_operating_screen.py`, and
`merge_3d_mot_single_pass_operating_screen.py` evaluated a 15-point grid:
blue detunings `-2.75`, `-3.00`, and `-3.25 Gamma` crossed with blue `s0`
values 0.18, 0.22, 0.25, 0.28, and 0.32. Each point used 600 particles split
over three paired shards.

The walltime ended the run after 13 complete points; the `-3.25 Gamma` points
at `s0 = 0.28` and 0.32 were incomplete. The best complete point was
`-2.75 Gamma`, `s0 = 0.25`, with 310/600 usable atoms at 100 ms (51.7%). The
next complete point was `-3.00 Gamma`, `s0 = 0.32`, with 263/600 (43.8%). The
sharp non-monotonic response was retained as motivation for paired local
sensitivity checks. The Zeus-local result directory was
`data/validation/mot_3d/single_pass_yz_screen/local_refinement_600`.

## `yz` single-pass crossing-position screen

At blue `s0 = 0.25`, blue waist 7.5 mm and detuning `-2.75 Gamma`, paired
600-particle evaluations gave:

| Geometric crossing | Usable at 100 ms |
|---:|---:|
| -50 mm | 310/600 (51.7%) |
| -45 mm | 279/600 (46.5%) |
| -40 mm | 272/600 (45.3%) |

The nearby positions remained viable, but -50 mm was best at that tested
operating point. Separate `-3 Gamma` evaluations were much poorer (16/600,
52/600 and 64/600 at -50, -45 and -40 mm respectively), while `-2.5 Gamma`
gave 9/600 at all three positions. This demonstrated strong detuning-position
coupling and motivated optimizing the crossing position while deriving the
blue detuning from a physically explicit slowing reference.

## `yz` single-pass slowing-location diagnostic

For the -50 mm crossing, 60/120 particles were captured and the median
peak-slowing position for captured atoms was -57.96 mm. For the -45 mm
crossing, 61/120 were captured and the median was -53.32 mm. Missed atoms had
similar peak locations (-58.83 and -53.69 mm). Thus peak slowing occurred about
8 mm upstream of the geometric crossing, not exactly at it. This is why the
canonical model distinguishes geometric crossing position from effective
slowing position when applying the Zeeman-aware blue-detuning correction.

## Reconstruction policy

Retired exploratory programs remain available in Git history. Reintroduce one
only if its scientific question becomes active again; do not copy an old
submitter into a new campaign. New work must use the canonical campaign
manager, frozen manifests, and current corrected 2D-MOT survivor inputs.
